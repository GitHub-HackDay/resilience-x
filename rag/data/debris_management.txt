Debris Management and Removal

Post-disaster debris removal is essential for public safety and community recovery:

Debris Categories:
- Vegetative debris (trees, branches, landscaping materials)
- Construction and demolition debris (building materials, concrete, metals)
- Household hazardous waste (paint, chemicals, batteries)
- White goods (appliances, furniture, electronics)
- Vehicles and vessels damaged beyond repair

Collection Strategy:
- Establish temporary debris storage and reduction sites outside flood zones
- Deploy collection crews systematically by neighborhood zones
- Separate debris types for recycling and proper disposal
- Document debris quantities and locations with GPS for FEMA reimbursement

Safety Protocols:
- All crews must wear personal protective equipment
- Identify and properly handle hazardous materials
- Coordinate with utilities before removing debris near power lines
- Screen for cultural or historical artifacts in affected areas

Debris removal operations typically take 6-12 months to complete after major disasters, with priority given to clearing major roads and public facilities first.
